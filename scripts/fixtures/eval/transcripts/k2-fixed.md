To achieve the goal that China and Nepal jointly announce the new height of Mount Qomolangma (MQ), the campaign of Qomolangma Height Survey (QHS) was carried out from 2019 to 2020. A high precision geoid model realizing the common height datum for both sides is necessary for determining the unique height of MQ. However, high altitude and rugged topography make it extremely difficult to conduct terrestrial gravity measurements in this region, the accuracy of geoid model is restricted by terrestrial gravity data gaps. In the campaign of 2020 QHS, the airborne gravity survey was carried out over MQ and its surrounding areas, the airborne gravity data covering an area of 12,700 km2 were successfully collected. For the first time, the high precision observations of terrestrial gravity and BeiDou Navigation Satellite System (BDS) at the peak of MQ were collected. These datasets pave the way for the precise determination of the orthometric height of MQ. According to the definition of the International Height Reference System (IHRS), we developed the IHRS-based gravimetric quasigeoid model by combining the airborne and terrestrial gravity data. Validations against highly accurate GNSS leveling data at 61 benchmarks demonstrate that the accuracy of the quasigeoid model is 3.8 cm, and the addition of airborne gravity data improves the accuracy by 51.3%. Based on the IHRS, the final orthometric height of the snow surface of the peak of MQ is determined to be 8848.86 m.

Chomolungma, also known as Mount Everest or Sagarmāthā (Fig. 1a), is the highest mountain on Earth, towering over other peaks in the Himalayas (Fig. 1b). At 8,849 m above sea level, Chomolungma is ~250 m higher than the other tallest peaks in the Himalaya. This is surprising given the relative along-strike uniformity of tectonics in the Himalayas, which provides mountain peak buoyancy with local, small-scale variability 1, 2, and relatively uniform climatic conditions and erosional processes 3, 4. Despite this, Chomolungma deviates from the linear trend of the elevation of Himalayan peaks plotted against their elevation rank (Fig. 1b). A simplistic analysis hints that this anomalous elevation (on the order of one to two hundred metres) could be the result of a scenario where peak elevations were relatively uniformly distributed, but an additional component of elevation has been added to only some of the peaks (including the highest peak). Only the highest of these affected peaks would disrupt the linear trend, whereas lower peaks affected by the additional elevation simply move up the ranking (Fig. 1b). ## The top of Mount Chimborazo is farther from the Earth's center than Mount Everest.

 The highest point above Earth’s center is the peak of Ecuador’s Mount Chimborazo, located just one degree south of the Equator where Earth’s bulge is greatest. | Infographic Text

Mount Everest, located in Nepal and Tibet, is usually said to be the highest mountain on Earth. Reaching 8,848 meters at its summit, Everest is indeed the highest point above global mean sea level—the average level for the ocean surface from which elevations are measured. But the summit of Mt. Everest is not the farthest point from Earth’s center.

Earth is not a perfect sphere, but is a bit thicker at the Equator due to the centrifugal force created by the planet’s constant rotation. Because of this, the highest point above Earth’s center is the peak of Ecuador’s Mount Chimborazo, located just one degree south of the Equator where Earth’s bulge is greatest. The summit of Chimborazo is 6,268 meters above sea level. However, due to the Earth’s bulge, the summit of Chimborazo is over 2,072 meters farther from the center of the Earth than Everest’s peak. That makes Chimborazo the closest point on Earth to the stars. 

You may be surprised to learn that Everest is not the tallest mountain on Earth, either. That honor belongs to Mauna Kea, a volcano on the Big Island of Hawaii. Mauna Kea originates deep beneath the Pacific Ocean, and rises more than 10,210 meters from base to peak.

A complete high-altitude gear list for a K2 expedition is organized below by system. 

*(Note: While most group gear like high-camp tents, ropes, and stoves are provided by your expedition outfitter, all personal clothing, boots, and technical climbing hardware must be brought by you.)*

---

### 1. Footwear & Feet System
* **8,000-Meter Expedition Boots:** Purpose-built double or triple extreme-altitude boots with removable inner liners (e.g., La Sportiva Olympus Mons Cube or Scarpa Phantom 8000).
* **Approach / Trekking Boots:** Sturdy, broken-in waterproof boots for the 7–10 day Baltoro Glacier trek to Base Camp.
* **Camp Boots:** Comfortable, warm boots or booties for lounging around Base Camp and high camps.
* **Socks:** 3 pairs of lightweight synthetic sock liners and 6 pairs of heavy-weight mountaineering wool socks.
* **Gaiters:** Waterproof mountaineering gaiters (if not integrated into your 8,000m boots).

---

### 2. Clothing & Outerwear (Layering System)
* **Base Layers:** 2–3 pairs of synthetic or merino wool long-sleeve tops and long bottoms (light-to-medium weight).
* **Mid Layers:** 1 heavy thermal top/bottom, active soft-shell pants, and a breathable soft-shell jacket.
* **Hard Shells:** Waterproof, breathable Gore-Tex (or equivalent) hard-shell jacket with a hood and matching hard-shell pants with full-length side zips.
* **Insulation Layers (Lower Camps):** Heavyweight down jacket with a hood and insulated synthetic pants for use below Camp 2.
* **The Summit Down Suit:** A professional, 1-piece 8,000-meter expedition down suit (mandatory for the death zone above Camp 2/3).

---

### 3. Headwear & Handwear
* **Warm Hats:** 1 wool/synthetic beanie.
* **Balaclava / Face Mask:** Windproof balaclava to protect against high-altitude winds and frostbite.
* **Sun Protection:** Baseball cap or wide-brim sun hat and a UV-blocking neck buff.
* **Gloves:** 
  * Lightweight synthetic liner gloves.
  * Mid-weight soft-shell alpine climbing gloves.
  * Heavy insulated waterproof gloves.
* **Expedition Down Mittens:** Extremely warm, oversized down mitts large enough to fit over liner gloves.

---

### 4. Technical Climbing Kit
* **Crampons:** 12-point vertical-point steel technical crampons with anti-balling plates, precision-fitted to your 8,000m boots.
* **Ice Axe:** Technical mixed-alpine ice axe (with hammer/adze configurations as specified by your guide).
* **Climbing Helmet:** UIAA-certified alpine helmet spacious enough to fit over a beanie/balaclava.
* **Harness:** Lightweight, adjustable alpine climbing harness with easy-to-manipulate gear loops (accessible while wearing bulky mittens).
* **Ascender & Hardware:** 1 hand ascender (Jumar) with a safety lanyard, several locking and non-locking carabiners, an ATC/reverso belay device, and a rappel device (Figure-8).
* **Trekking Poles:** Adjustable carbon or aluminum poles with snow baskets for the Baltoro approach.

---

### 5. Sleep & Camp Systems
* **High-Camp Sleeping Bag:** Goose-down bag rated to at least **-40°F / -40°C** for camps above 6,000m.
* **Base Camp Sleeping Bag:** Goose-down or synthetic bag rated to at least **-20°F** for comfortable recovery at Base Camp.
* **Sleeping Pads (Dual System):** 1 full-length insulated self-inflating pad (e.g., Therm-a-Rest NeoAir) *plus* 1 closed-cell foam pad for high-camp ground insulation.
* **Pee Bottle:** 1-liter wide-mouth bottle designated for tent use at night.
* **Eating Utensils:** Insulated mug/cup, bowl, and unbreakable spoon.

---

### 6. Eyewear & Sun Protection
* **Glacier Glasses:** Category 4 dark lenses with side shields to prevent severe snow blindness.
* **Ski Goggles:** High-altitude goggles (preferably with an interchangeable or amber lens) for high winds and summit day.
* **Sunscreen & Lip Balm:** High-SPF (50+) sports sunscreen and multiple tubes of high-SPF lip balm.

---

### 7. Electronics & Travel Logistics
* **Headlamps:** 2 high-lumen headlamps (e.g., Petzl Actik/Tactikka Core) with plenty of spare batteries or charging cables.
* **Power & Charging:** Personal power bank system (solar panels or high-capacity batteries) since electricity at Base Camp is limited.
